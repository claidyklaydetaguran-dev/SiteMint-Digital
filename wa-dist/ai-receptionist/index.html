Appointments and calendar

The full appointment lifecycle

Certified on staging; customer controls arriving in the private beta.

  1. 01

    Availability check

    The receptionist checks real open slots against your calendar and rules.

  2. 02

    Request

    A caller's preferred time is captured as a request, not an automatic booking.

  3. 03

    Approve / book

    A request is reviewed and confirmed, moving it onto the calendar.

  4. 04

    Reschedule

    A booked appointment can be moved to a new time with the change tracked.

  5. 05

    Cancel

    A booking can be cancelled, freeing the slot and closing out the record.

  6. 06

    Google Calendar availability

    Availability reflects your connected Google Calendar, not a static schedule.

Caller examples

What a conversation looks like

Three short, simulated exchanges — illustrative, not recordings of real calls.

Simulated example — appointment request

Caller“Hi, do you have anything open this week?”

Assistant“I can check. What day works best, and is there a time of day you'd prefer?”

Caller“Thursday afternoon, if possible.”

Assistant“Got it — I'll put in a request for Thursday afternoon and the team will confirm with you.”

Simulated example — routine question

Caller“What are your hours?”

Assistant“We're open weekdays, nine to six. Is there anything else I can help with?”

Caller“That's all, thanks.”

Assistant“You're welcome — have a good day.”

Simulated example — handoff

Caller“This is actually kind of urgent, I need to speak with someone directly.”

Assistant“Understood — I'll pass this to the team right away with what you've told me so far.”

Safe failure

What the receptionist won't attempt on its own

Every account sets explicit boundaries: topics it won't address, actions it won't take, and the situations — an upset caller, a request outside scope, anything ambiguous — where it hands off to a person with the conversation's context instead of guessing.

  • Outside its script → Hands off with context
  • Caller sounds upset → Hands off with context
  • Request is ambiguous → Hands off instead of guessing
Privacy and retention

What's kept, and what isn't

SiteMint does not retain call audio or full transcripts. The dashboard stores only the operational call details and outcomes needed to manage the receptionist.

Built for different businesses

Where it fits

The same underlying receptionist, configured differently for how each business actually uses its phone.

Professional services

Fielding routine questions and appointment requests so staff time goes to the work itself, not the phone.

Legal offices

Capturing the basics of an inquiry — what it's about and how soon it's needed — before a person picks it up.

Real estate teams

Handling incoming questions about availability and coordinating a next step while an agent is with a client.

Home services

Answering calls about scheduling and service questions when the team is on a job site, not at a desk.

Healthcare offices

Handling routine scheduling questions only, within the same no-audio, no-transcript retention policy as every other business — sensitive clinical conversations still route to staff.

Appointment-based businesses

Any business whose calls are mostly about booking, changing, or confirming a time.

FAQ

Straight answers

Is this live today?

The Interactive Preview above is a real, working simulation you can try right now — it makes no call and stores nothing. Live call answering is still being certified and is not yet advertised as active.

What happens to call data?

SiteMint does not retain call audio or full transcripts. The dashboard stores only the operational call details and outcomes needed to manage the receptionist.

How much does it cost?

Private-beta pricing is provided during onboarding.

Can I sign up right now?

The AI Receptionist is invite-only during private beta. Requesting access starts the conversation — it does not create an account immediately.

What if the receptionist can't handle something?

Every account defines what the receptionist should never attempt and when a call should be handed to a person instead — see the safe-failure section below.

Which businesses is this built for?

Any business whose calls are mostly routine questions and appointment coordination — see the use-cases section for specifics.
